Craft cheat sheet

Rap
Multisyllabic rhyme
Rhyme two or three syllables at once: broken heart / open art. The Rhyme lab's "multis" find them.
Internal rhyme
Rhymes inside the line, not just at the end: stack the facts, back to back.
Punchline
Set up an image, then flip it in the last two words, landing on the snare.
Flow switch
Change rhythm mid-verse: from 8th notes to triplets, or start lines off the beat.
The pocket
Where your syllables sit against the drums. Aim the last stressed syllable at the snare on 4.
Poetry
Meter
The pattern of stressed (●) and unstressed (○) syllables. Iambs ○●, trochees ●○, anapests ○○●, dactyls ●○○.
Enjambment
Let a sentence run over the line break so the reader leans into the next line.
Caesura
A pause in the middle of a line (a comma, a dash): I hold the hands — of strangers.
The volta
The turn: the place a poem changes its mind. Sonnets often turn at line 9.
Slant rhyme
Rhymes that almost match: rain / same, saucer / daughter. Quieter than perfect rhyme.
Pop
The hook
The line people sing back. Short, repeated, usually the title.
Pre-chorus lift
Shorter lines and rising tension right before the chorus "opens up".
Prosody
Put stressed syllables on strong beats, and the emotional word on the longest note.
Open vowels
Ooh, ay, oh, ah are easy to hold and belt. Save them for long notes.
Contrast
Verse low and wordy, chorus high and simple. The change is what feels big.
